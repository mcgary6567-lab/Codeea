Spot vs Futures Trading Bots: Which Should You Run?

📅 August 25, 2026⏱ 7 min read
By the Prometheus AI Team

The same bot strategy behaves very differently on spot versus futures — mostly because of one word: leverage. It magnifies gains and losses equally, and it introduces liquidation risk that spot simply doesn't have. Here's how to choose.

Spot has no liquidation risk; futures adds leverage that magnifies both gains and losses and can liquidate you. Beginners should almost always start on spot.

Spot Trading Bots

On spot, you own the actual coin. A bot buys and sells real assets, and the worst case is the asset's value falling — there's no liquidation. It's simpler, lower-risk, and where most people should start.

The trade-off: no leverage, so returns track the size of your capital.

Futures Trading Bots

On futures, you trade contracts with leverage — controlling a larger position than your capital. This magnifies gains and losses, and if price moves against you far enough, your position is liquidated (closed at a loss automatically).

Futures also involve funding fees paid periodically between longs and shorts.

The Leverage Reality Most People Underestimate

Leverage isn't a profit multiplier — it's a risk multiplier. At 10× leverage, a 10% move against you can wipe the position. A bot that's disciplined on spot can be dangerous on high leverage with the same signals.

If you use futures, keep leverage low and let the bot's risk controls do their job.

Which Should You Run?

Start on spot. It's simpler and can't liquidate you. Move to futures only once you understand leverage and are comfortable with the extra risk — and even then, keep leverage low. Prometheus supports both spot and futures across major exchanges, so you can start safe and scale up deliberately.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I run a trading bot on spot or futures?
Most people should start on spot: you own the actual coin, there's no leverage and no liquidation risk. Futures adds leverage that magnifies both gains and losses and can liquidate your position, so it's for experienced users who keep leverage low.
Is a futures trading bot riskier than spot?
Yes. Futures uses leverage, which multiplies losses as well as gains and can liquidate your position if price moves against you. Spot has no liquidation — the worst case is the asset falling in value.
Can the same bot trade both spot and futures?
Many bots, including Prometheus, support both. The strategy logic can be similar, but the risk is very different because of leverage — so size and leverage settings matter far more on futures.
