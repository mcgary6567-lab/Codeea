First, What an EMA Is

An Exponential Moving Average (EMA) is the average price over a period, weighted toward recent prices so it reacts faster than a simple average. A fast EMA (like 9) hugs price closely; a slow EMA (like 21) is smoother and lags a little.

When the fast EMA is above the slow one, short-term momentum is up; when it's below, momentum is down. That relationship is the heart of the strategy.

The 9/21 Crossover Signal

The rule is simple:

  • Bullish cross: the 9-EMA crosses above the 21-EMA → momentum turning up → a potential long.
  • Bearish cross: the 9-EMA crosses below the 21-EMA → momentum turning down → a potential short or exit.

On its own, though, this signal fires constantly — including in sideways markets where it just chops you up. That's the trap.

Why the Trend Filter Is the Real Secret

The fix is a longer-term trend filter — for example, a 100-EMA. You only take crossovers in the direction of the bigger trend:

  • Price above the 100-EMA? Take bullish crosses only.
  • Price below the 100-EMA? Take bearish crosses only.

This one rule filters out most of the losing whipsaws, because you stop fighting the dominant trend. Prometheus's managed strategy uses exactly this: a 9/21 cross confirmed by a 100-EMA trend filter.

Entries, Stops and Exits

  • Entry: on a confirmed cross in the trend direction (a confirmation candle avoids acting on a fake-out).
  • Stop-loss: beyond the recent swing — structural, not a random number — sized so the loss is a small fixed percentage of the account.
  • Take-profit: a fixed reward-to-risk target, often with a partial take-profit and a trailing stop to let winners run.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the 9/21 EMA crossover strategy?
It's a momentum strategy: go long when the 9-period EMA crosses above the 21-period EMA, and exit or go short when it crosses below. It works best when filtered by a longer-term trend EMA so you only trade crossovers in the direction of the dominant trend.
Is the EMA crossover strategy profitable in crypto?
It can be when combined with a trend filter and strict risk control, but never guaranteed. A raw crossover whipsaws in sideways markets; adding a 100-EMA trend filter and a fixed-risk stop is what gives it a realistic edge.
Why add a trend filter to an EMA crossover?
Because a bare crossover fires constantly, including in choppy markets where it loses. A longer-term trend filter keeps you trading only with the dominant trend, cutting out most of the losing whipsaws.
