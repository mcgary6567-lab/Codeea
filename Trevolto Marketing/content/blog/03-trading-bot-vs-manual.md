# Crypto Trading Bot vs. Manual Trading: Which Is Right for You?

Should you trade crypto by hand or let a bot do it? Neither is "better" in the
abstract — it depends on your time, temperament, and discipline. Here's an honest
breakdown.

## The quick comparison

| | Manual trading | Trading bot |
|---|---|---|
| **Availability** | When you're awake & watching | 24/7 |
| **Emotion** | Fear/greed creep in | Follows rules exactly |
| **Speed** | Human reaction time | Instant entries, stops, targets |
| **Flexibility** | Adapt to news on the fly | Only does what it's told |
| **Discipline** | Depends on you | Built into the rules |
| **Learning curve** | Charting & psychology | Defining solid rules + setup |

## When manual trading makes sense

- You trade discretionary setups that are hard to codify.
- You want to react to breaking news and context.
- You enjoy the process and have the screen time.

The catch: manual trading lives or dies by *your* discipline. Most blown accounts
come from oversizing and revenge trading — both human, both avoidable with rules.

## When a bot makes sense

- You have a clear, rule-based strategy.
- You can't (or don't want to) watch charts all day and night.
- You want every trade to carry the same risk and the same stop/target.
- You want to remove emotion from execution.

The catch: a bot only follows the rules you give it. Garbage rules → garbage
results. It still needs monitoring and periodic review.

## Risk management matters either way

Whichever you choose, the fundamentals are identical:
- Risk a small, fixed % per trade.
- Use stop-losses — always.
- Set a daily-loss limit and walk away when it's hit.
- Keep custody of your funds; use API keys with **withdrawals disabled**.

## A practical middle ground

Many traders do both: trade discretionary ideas by hand, and **automate a
proven, rule-based strategy** so it runs in the background 24/7. That's where a
desktop tool like [Trevolto](https://trevolto.com) helps — it runs your automated
strategy on your own PC with hard risk limits, a Safe Mode to paper-trade, and a
one-click PANIC button, while you keep full custody of your keys.

## Bottom line

Choose manual if your edge is discretionary and you have the time and discipline.
Choose a bot if your edge is rule-based and you want consistency without the
all-nighters. Either way, risk management — not the method — is what keeps you in
the game.
