How grid and martingale EAs work

A grid EA opens another position every time price moves a set distance against it. A martingale also multiplies the lot size each time. When price turns back a little, the whole basket closes in profit. Nearly every trade “wins”, the balance curve rises in a straight line, and the losses sit unrealised in open positions.

The maths of one gold spike

Gold moves in dollars. On one standard lot, every $1 move is about $100. Take a martingale that starts at 0.01 lot and doubles every $3 against it:

How to spot a grid EA from its listing

  1. Read the full report, not the screenshot. Compare the average loss with the average win. A martingale shows tiny wins and rare, enormous losses – or no closed losses at all in a short test.
  2. Compare balance and equity curves. A straight balance line with deep equity dips means losses are being carried, not taken.
  3. Check maximum open positions and lot growth in the inputs and in the trade list.
  4. Test the worst months, not the last month. Run the EA through a big news period on real ticks.
  5. Distrust 90%+ win rates with no stop loss mentioned.

FAQ

Why do martingale gold EAs have such smooth equity curves?

Because they hide losing trades by adding bigger positions instead of closing them. Small wins keep printing until a move is large enough that the stacked positions cannot recover, and the account loses months of gains in hours.

How do I know if an EA uses a grid or martingale?

Look for no stop loss, many positions open at once, lot sizes that grow after a loss, an average loss far larger than the average win in the full report, and a balance curve that is perfectly straight while the equity curve dips deeply.

Is Gold Scalpers Pro a grid EA?
