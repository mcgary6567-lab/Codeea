How to Pass a Prop Firm Challenge Trading Gold (XAUUSD)

📅 July 7, 2026🔄 Updated November 13, 2026⏱ 15 min read
By Gold Scalpers Research Team · Firm rules checked on each firm's own pages, November 13, 2026

A prop firm challenge is rarely lost on the entries. It is lost when one risk rule breaks on one candle. Gold (XAUUSD) makes that easy: it can cover an 8–10% target in days, and it can take 5% off an over-sized account in one news release. This guide covers the arithmetic of a gold challenge, how firms really measure the daily loss, the lot sizes for $10k, $25k, $50k and $100k accounts, and what four large firms say about running an Expert Advisor.

The three numbers of every challenge

Every evaluation is three numbers. You have to reach the first before touching either of the other two:

  • Profit target, usually 8% or 10% of the starting balance.
  • Daily loss limit, usually 3% to 5%.
  • Maximum loss, usually 6% to 10%.

Those are not round guesses. This is what four large firms published for their main programs when we checked them on November 13, 2026:

How the daily loss is really measured

This is the rule that ends most gold challenges, and most traders have it wrong. The daily limit is not “how much you have lost in closed trades today”.

  • FTMO takes your balance at midnight (CET/CEST), subtracts a fixed percentage of the initial account, and your equity may not fall below that line. Equity includes open trades, swaps and commissions (FTMO trading objectives).
  • FundedNext uses a fixed dollar amount based on the initial balance. Floating profit and loss, swaps and commissions all count, and the day resets at 00:00 server time (FundedNext daily loss article).
  • The5ers measures from the higher of the day's starting balance or starting equity, at MT5 server time (The5ers High Stakes).
  • FundingPips uses “the higher value between the opening balance or opening equity for that day”, and equity may not fall by more than the limit, “including floating losses from open trades” (FundingPips 2 Step Standard).

A worked example on a $100,000 FTMO 2-Step account. You start the day with a balance of $102,000. Your line for today is $102,000 minus $5,000 = $97,000 of equity. In the morning you close two winners for +$1,500, so your balance is $103,500. In the afternoon you hold a gold position through a data release and it runs $6,500 against you. Your equity is now $97,000. You have breached the daily limit while your closed result for the day is still positive, and before your stop was even hit.

The daily limit is an equity line that includes your open trade. A stop that is too far away for your lot size is a breach waiting for a fast candle.

Why gold accounts fail challenges

  • Lot size chosen first, stop second. On gold, one standard lot moves $100 for every $1 of price. Two lots with a $12 stop is $2,400 of risk, which is 2.4% of a $100k account in one trade.
  • News. US CPI, non-farm payrolls and Fed decisions can move gold $20 to $40 in minutes, with spreads several times wider than normal. See how US data and FOMC move gold.
  • The daily rollover. Around the end of the New York session liquidity thins, spreads widen and stops get hit by prices that never trade in normal hours. The best time to trade gold is the London and New York overlap, not the last hour of the day.
  • Trying to win it back. One loss is inside the plan. A larger second trade to recover it is how a 1% loss becomes a 5% day.

Five rules that keep you in the challenge

  1. Risk 0.5% to 1% per trade. On a 5% daily limit, 1% risk gives you five full losers in a day before a breach. On a 3% daily limit, use 0.5%. Start with the 1% rule.
  2. Stop yourself before the firm does. Set a personal daily stop at about 60% of the firm's limit: 3% against a 5% limit, 1.5% to 2% against a 3% limit. When you reach it, you are done for the day.
  3. Keep a floor above the maximum loss. If the maximum loss is 10%, treat 8% as the end. The last 2% is there for slippage and gaps, not for trading.
  4. No new trades around high-impact US news or the rollover. Some firms forbid it on funded accounts anyway (see the EA section below).
  5. Aim for ordinary days. FTMO's 1-Step has a best-day rule (no single day above 50% of total profit), and every firm above requires several trading days. One large day does not pass a challenge.

The lot size falls as the stop widens, and the dollar risk stays the same. Add the spread and commission to the stop distance before you divide: on a $2 stop, a 30-cent spread is 15% more risk than the table shows. Check the margin too. Five lots is 500 ounces of gold. The5ers gives 1:25 leverage on metals in High Stakes, so the margin is 4% of the value of the position, which can be most of a $100k account. The tight-stop rows are not practical at every firm.

Which size should you buy? The rules are the same percentages at every size, so the trading is identical. A smaller account costs less to fail and less to repeat. Buy the size whose fee you could pay three times without it changing how you trade.

The pace plan: about 27 trades, and the streak you must survive

Assume you risk 1% per trade (call it 1R), take profit at 1.5R, and win 55% of the time. Those are assumptions, not a forecast. The average trade is then worth:

(0.55 × 1.5R) − (0.45 × 1R) = 0.375R

A 10% target is 10R, so it takes about 27 trades on average. An 8% target takes about 21. At two or three clean setups a day that is two to three weeks, which is why the minimum-day rules rarely matter for a patient trader.

The same assumptions tell you what will go wrong on the way. Across 27 trades with a 45% chance of losing each one:

Losing streakChance of seeing it at least once in 27 tradesCost at 1% risk
3 losses in a rowabout 80%−3%
4 losses in a rowabout 46%−4%
5 losses in a rowabout 22%−5%

Three losses in a row is close to certain. Five in a row happens in roughly one challenge out of five, and at 1% risk five losses on the same day is exactly a 5% daily breach. That is the whole reason for the personal daily stop at three losers: it turns the streak you will almost certainly meet into a bad day, not a failed account.

Running an EA on a challenge: what the firms say

An Expert Advisor does not get tired or try to win a loss back, which removes the most common ways to fail. But the firms do not treat EAs alike, and a bought EA is treated differently from one you wrote. This is what their own pages said on November 13, 2026:

The5ersNoIts prohibited list includes “Using an EA from a provider where the trader does not own the source code”. A compiled EA you bought does not meet that (prohibited practices).
FundingPipsNoA third-party EA is “permitted only when used strictly as a trade or risk manager”. Full automation is allowed only with an EA you developed yourself, with proof of ownership, and “a compiled binary on its own is not proof” (conduct standards).

Rules change, and a firm's support desk has the final word on your account. Read the current page and, if you plan to run any EA, ask support in writing before you pay the fee. There is a longer comparison in prop firms that allow EA trading.

Frequently asked questions

How much do I need to make to pass a $100k prop firm challenge?

On a 10% program, $10,000, without your equity ever falling $5,000 below the day's starting balance or $10,000 below the starting balance. On an 8% program the target is $8,000. Two-step programs then ask for a second, smaller target, usually 5%.

What lot size should I use on gold in a challenge?

The one that makes your stop equal to 0.5% to 1% of the account. Lots = risk in dollars ÷ (stop distance in dollars × 100). On a $10,000 account with a $5 stop and 1% risk that is 0.20 lot; on $100,000 it is 2.00 lots.

Is the daily loss limit based on balance or equity?

Equity at the firms we checked. FTMO sets the line from the midnight balance and counts open positions against it; FundedNext counts floating profit and loss, swaps and commissions; The5ers and FundingPips measure from the higher of the day's starting balance or equity.

How long does it take to pass?

With 1% risk, a 1.5 to 1 reward and a 55% win rate, a 10% target takes about 27 trades on average, usually two to three weeks. Most firms have removed time limits, and they require three to five trading days per phase.

Can I pass a prop firm challenge with an EA?

At some firms. FTMO allows EAs with conditions, FundedNext allows them only on accounts under $50,000 for a fee, and The5ers and FundingPips do not allow a purchased EA to trade the account. No EA can guarantee a pass.

Which account size should I start with?

The smallest one whose fee you could afford to lose several times. The percentage rules are the same at every size, so the skill carries over, and the cost of learning is lower.

The short version

Know your program's three numbers in dollars. Remember that the daily limit is an equity line that includes your open trade. Let the stop set the lot size, stop your own day at three losers, stay out of the news and the rollover, and expect a losing streak on the way to the target. If you want software to hold those limits for you, check the firm's EA policy first.
