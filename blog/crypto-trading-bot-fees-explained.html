Crypto Trading Bot Fees Explained: What You Actually Pay

📅 August 25, 2026⏱ 6 min read
By the Prometheus AI Team

There are two kinds of cost with any trading bot: what you pay the bot, and what you pay the exchange. Most people only look at the first. Here's the full picture so nothing surprises you.

A one-time bot fee beats a monthly subscription over time — but the cost that actually adds up is exchange trading fees, so favor a bot that isn't over-trading.

1. What You Pay the Bot

Bots charge in two models: a recurring subscription (monthly/annual) or a one-time payment for lifetime access. Over a year or two, one-time is usually far cheaper — a $30/month bot is $360/year, every year.

2. Exchange Trading Fees (The Real Cost)

Every trade the bot makes pays your exchange's maker/taker fee — typically ~0.02%–0.1% per side. This is separate from the bot, and it adds up fast if a bot over-trades.

A bot that fires dozens of trades a day can quietly cost more in exchange fees than a disciplined one that takes a few quality setups.

3. The Costs People Forget: Spread & Funding

  • Spread — the gap between buy and sell price; wider on illiquid coins and during news.
  • Funding rate (futures only) — a periodic payment between longs and shorts for holding a position.

A good bot includes a spread guard so it doesn't trade into expensive conditions.

How to Keep Fees Low

  • Prefer a one-time bot over a subscription.
  • Use a bot that trades selectively, not constantly.
  • Check your exchange's fee tier — some discount for holding their token or higher volume.
  • Avoid illiquid coins with wide spreads.

A Simple Example

Say the bot takes 4 trades/day at 0.05% taker fee on a $1,000 position: that's ~$0.40/day in exchange fees, or ~$12/month — regardless of the bot's own price. Now imagine a bot that takes 40 trades/day: same math, 10× the cost. Trade quality matters more than trade quantity.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do crypto trading bots charge fees?
Bots charge either a subscription or a one-time fee. Separately, you always pay your exchange's trading fees on each order the bot places — that exchange cost is often the larger one over time.
Is a one-time bot cheaper than a subscription?
Usually, yes. A one-time payment for lifetime access avoids the recurring cost of a monthly subscription, which keeps adding up every year you use it.
What's the biggest hidden cost of a trading bot?
Exchange trading fees on a bot that over-trades, plus spread on illiquid coins. A disciplined bot that takes fewer, higher-quality trades usually costs less in fees than one that trades constantly.
