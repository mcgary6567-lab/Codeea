How Much Do Solo Ads Cost in 2026?

Real prices per click, what you actually pay per subscriber, and how much to put into your first test.

By Ihsan · November 17, 2026 · 8 min read

Short answer: solo ads cost roughly $0.17 to $0.87 per click in 2026. Most good Tier-1 lists sit somewhere between $0.30 and $0.70. A typical first order is $100 to $200, which gets you 200 to 600 clicks depending on who you buy from.

That's the number people ask for. It's also the number that tricks the most beginners, because price per click on its own tells you very little. A cheap click that nobody opts in from is the most expensive click you can buy.

So below I'll give you the real prices I see today, then show you the number you should actually care about, which is what each subscriber costs you.

What solo ads cost right now

Udimi is the biggest solo ads marketplace, so it's a good place to read the market. On November 17, 2026 I opened the first page of its seller directory (make money online niche) and wrote down the prices:

  • Lowest: $0.17 per click
  • Middle price: $0.45 per click
  • Highest: $0.87 per click
  • A seller rated 4.9 (1,885 ratings): $0.69 per click

Only the first page of results, so treat it as a snapshot, not a survey. Sellers change their prices often.

The number that matters: cost per subscriber

You're not really buying clicks. You're buying people on your email list. The formula is simple:

Cost per subscriber = price per click ÷ opt-in rate

Here's what that does to the prices above. I've used a 35% and a 45% opt-in rate because that's the range our own list averages on funnels we've reviewed. Your rate will depend on your page, and it can be a lot lower if the page is weak.

What makes one solo ad cost more than another

Where the clicks come from

Tier-1 traffic (US, UK, Canada, Australia) costs more almost everywhere. Readers in those countries tend to buy more online, so sellers can charge more. If your offer only converts in those countries, pay for Tier-1. If it sells worldwide, a mixed list can work fine and cost less. There's more on this in Tier-1 vs Tier-2 traffic.

How the list was built

A list of people who've bought something before is worth more than a list of freebie seekers. Good sellers will tell you roughly how their list was built and how often they mail it. If a seller won't say, that tells you something too.

Your niche

Make money online has the most sellers to pick from (Udimi's homepage shows over a thousand active sellers in its marketing niche), so prices stay competitive. Narrower niches like crypto, health or personal development have fewer sellers, and fewer sellers usually means higher prices.

Order size

Bigger orders usually get a lower price per click. You can see it in our own packages: 300 clicks cost $0.33 each, 1,100 cost $0.27.

What's included

Some prices are just clicks. Others include ad copy, a review of your squeeze page before sending, bonus clicks or a guarantee. A slightly higher price that includes a funnel check can save you an entire wasted run.

The costs people forget

The clicks aren't the whole bill. Before your first order, make sure you've budgeted for:

  • An email autoresponder. Without one you can't follow up, and most solo ad sales happen in the follow-up, not on day one. See autoresponders that work with solo ads.
  • A squeeze page. Many autoresponders include a page builder, so this can be free.
  • A click tracker. You need your own numbers, not just the seller's. Here's how to track solo ad clicks.
  • A test budget. Plan for at least two or three small runs before you judge the channel. One run is a coin flip.

How much should you spend on your first solo ad?

Around $100. At today's prices that buys 200 to 300 clicks, which is enough to see your real opt-in rate and spot obvious problems with your funnel. It isn't enough to judge profit, so don't expect to.

Here's the order I'd do things in:

  1. Get your squeeze page and a short follow-up sequence ready first.
  2. Buy a small run of about $100.
  3. Check your cost per subscriber after the run finishes.
  4. If it's acceptable, run the same list again or go a size up.
  5. Judge profit after 30 days of follow-up emails, not on the day of the run.

Are cheap solo ads a trap?

Not always. Some low-priced sellers are new and building their reputation, and some are just in a cheaper niche. But a low price is no evidence of quality, and there's less room in the price for the seller to keep their list clean.

Before you buy cheap, check three things: the seller's ratings, their Tier-1 percentage, and whether you can track the clicks yourself. If any of those is missing, keep the order small. These red flags for bad vendors are worth a read too.

Bottom line

Expect to pay $0.30 to $0.70 a click for decent Tier-1 solo ads in 2026, start with around $100, and judge every seller on cost per subscriber and 30-day sales rather than the sticker price. If you'd like a second opinion on your funnel before you spend anything, send it over. Reviews are free.
