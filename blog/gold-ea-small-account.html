Gold EA for a Small Account: Can You Run One With $100 or $500?

📅 November 27, 2026⏱ 7 min read
By the Gold Scalpers developer · Lot maths for XAUUSD · Checked 2026-11-27

“Can I run a gold EA with $100?” is one of the questions we get most. The honest answer is not about the robot at all. It is about the smallest trade your broker lets you open and how far gold moves in a day. Once you do that sum, the right account size for any gold EA, ours included, becomes clear.

Short answer: you can, but not at the risk the backtests show. The smallest normal lot, 0.01, moves about $1 for every $1 gold moves. Gold Scalpers Pro’s backtests use 0.01 lot per $1,000, so on $500 the same trade carries twice the tested risk and on $100 it carries ten times. Use a cent account or a demo until you have about $1,000.

How much does a 0.01 lot of gold move?

A standard gold contract is 100 ounces. One full lot therefore gains or loses about $100 for every $1 move in XAUUSD, and 0.01 lot (one ounce) gains or loses about $1 per $1 move.

On most standard accounts 0.01 is also the minimum lot, and you cannot go below it. That is the whole small-account problem: the trade size has a floor, so the smaller your balance, the bigger each trade is as a share of it. Check your own broker’s numbers in MT5 by right-clicking XAUUSD in Market Watch and opening Specification; look at the contract size and the minimum volume, because a few brokers use different contract sizes.

How far does gold move in a normal day?

We measured the 2026 data on an Exness MT5 account from 5 January to 18 November. The average daily high-to-low range, month by month, ran from about $62 (October, the quietest month) to about $165 (March, the busiest). The busiest hours were 13:00–15:00 GMT; the XAUUSD volatility by hour breakdown has the detail.

At 0.01 lot, one average day of gold is a $62 to $165 swing. Here is that swing as a share of different balances:

An EA with a stop loss does not sit through the whole range, so these are not losses. They show why the stop has to be wide enough to survive normal noise, and why the dollar loss of one trade is not tiny even at the minimum lot. In Gold Scalpers Pro’s latest test month the average losing trade was $11.40 at 0.01 lot. On $1,000 that is about 1.1% of the account. On $100 it is 11.4% from a single ordinary loss, which breaks the 1% rule more than ten times over.

What does the backtest drawdown become on $100 or $500?

Gold Scalpers Pro’s eight-month backtest (12 January to 14 September 2026, 0.01 lot on $1,000) had a maximum drawdown of 25.6%. In money, that is roughly $256. At 0.01 lot the dollar drawdown stays about the same whatever you deposit, so the percentage grows as the account shrinks. The Low drawdown preset (New York session only) had a 10.3% maximum drawdown, about $103, over the same eight months.

The losing stretch inside that test makes the same point: from 12 January to 14 May the setup lost $181.94 at 0.01 lot. That is 18% of $1,000, 36% of $500, and more than an entire $100 account.

The EA’s 16% drawdown stop, with its 10% warning, limits a bad run, but it cannot make a 0.01 lot any smaller. On $100, one average losing trade already passes the 10% warning.

Can a cent account fix the small-account problem?

It can. Some brokers offer cent accounts, where the balance is shown in cents (a $100 deposit appears as 10,000) and the contract behind each lot is smaller, so a 0.01 “cent” lot moves far less than $1 per $1 in gold. That lets a small deposit trade at a sensible share of the balance.

Before you rely on one, check with your broker:

  • that cent accounts are available on MT5 and include gold (not every broker offers both);
  • the XAUUSD contract size and minimum lot in the symbol Specification, and whether the symbol name carries a suffix;
  • the typical gold spread on that account type, since a wide spread costs a scalper more than a small balance does.

Our published figures are $1,000 backtests at 0.01 lot, not cent-account tests, so run the EA on a cent demo first. The Gold Scalpers Pro licence is bound to your MT5 account number, so ask support to add a new cent account if you switch.

What is the realistic minimum for a gold EA?

For Gold Scalpers Pro at its tested sizing, the realistic minimum on a standard account is $1,000 per 0.01 lot. That is the only size the published drawdowns describe. At $2,000 with 0.01 lot you carry roughly half the tested risk, a more cautious starting point for a first live account. The settings guide has the full lot table up to $100,000.

Below $1,000 you have three honest options: a cent account, a demo account while you save, or accepting that the percentage swings will be two to ten times the backtest. Never use a small balance with a larger lot to “grow it faster”; that is how small gold accounts disappear in a single bad session.

How do you test your account size before risking money?

  1. Open the MT5 Strategy Tester, choose XAUUSD on M5 and set the deposit to your real balance, for example $500.
  2. Keep FixedLot at 0.01 and test several months on real ticks, including a losing stretch such as January to May 2026.
  3. Read the maximum drawdown and the lowest equity point, not just the final profit. Ask yourself whether you would really keep the EA running at that low.
  4. Forward-test the same settings on a demo account for a few weeks before going live.
