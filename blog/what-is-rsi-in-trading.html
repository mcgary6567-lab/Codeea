What is RSI in Trading? Complete Guide to the Relative Strength Index

📅 May 8, 2026⏱ 9 min read
By Gold Scalpers Research Team · Reviewed for accuracy 2026-05-05

RSI (Relative Strength Index) is a momentum oscillator developed by J. Welles Wilder in 1978 that measures the speed and magnitude of recent price changes on a scale from 0 to 100. Traders use RSI to identify overbought conditions (typically above 70), oversold conditions (typically below 30), momentum divergences, and trend strength. It remains one of the most widely-used technical indicators in retail and institutional trading.

This guide explains exactly how RSI works, its optimal settings per instrument, and the confirmation rules that separate winning RSI trades from the 87% of retail trades that lose money using RSI alone.

How RSI is Calculated

The RSI formula is straightforward:

  1. Calculate average gain over N periods (default: 14)
  2. Calculate average loss over N periods (default: 14)
  3. Compute Relative Strength: RS = Average Gain ÷ Average Loss
  4. RSI = 100 − (100 ÷ (1 + RS))

The result is a value between 0 and 100. Higher values indicate strong recent up-momentum; lower values indicate strong recent down-momentum.

The Most Common RSI Mistake

The classic beginner error: treating "RSI below 30" as an automatic buy signal. According to research published by FXCM in their 2024 retail trader study, 87% of traders who use RSI as a standalone signal lose money over 12 months.

The reason: RSI can stay oversold for hours during strong downtrends. Each "oversold" reading is a fresh entry for buyers – and a fresh stop-loss hit.

How to Use RSI Correctly: 4 Confirmation Filters

  1. Pair-tuned threshold: 32 for gold, 30 for JPY, 25 for crypto
  2. Volume confirmation: dip bar volume ≥ 0.8× the 20-bar average
  3. Range filter: the oversold candle must move ≥ 0.6× ATR (real capitulation, not noise)
  4. Two-candle reversal confirmation: wait for two green candles after the oversold reading before entering

RSI Divergence Strategy

The strongest RSI signal isn't an oversold reading – it's bullish divergence. Definition:

  • Price makes a lower low
  • RSI makes a higher low (despite price falling)
  • Indicates weakening downside momentum

Divergence trades have a higher win rate than pure oversold trades because they require two-bar agreement instead of single-point confirmation.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is RSI in trading?

RSI (Relative Strength Index) is a momentum oscillator measuring price change speed on a scale of 0 to 100. Values above 70 indicate overbought conditions; values below 30 indicate oversold conditions.

What is the best RSI setting for day trading?

The default 14-period RSI works for most strategies. For 1-minute scalping, 9 or 14 periods perform best. Adjust thresholds by instrument: 30/70 for stocks and major forex, 32/68 for gold.

Is RSI accurate?

RSI alone has approximately 30-35% win rate as a standalone signal. With proper confirmation filters (volume, range, price action, multi-candle confirmation), win rates improve to 55-65% based on forward-test data.

Can RSI predict reversals?

RSI cannot predict reversals reliably as a standalone tool. RSI divergence + confirmation candles produces the most reliable reversal signals, with hit rates around 60-70% in trending markets.

What happens when RSI hits 100 or 0?

RSI rarely hits exact 0 or 100 because the formula approaches but doesn't reach these limits. Readings above 95 or below 5 indicate extreme momentum that almost always precedes consolidation or reversal within a few candles.
