Are Solo Ads Worth It?

An honest answer from someone who sells them: when they pay off, when they don't, and how to find out for about $100.

By Ihsan · November 19, 2026 · 7 min read

Short answer: solo ads are worth it when you have three things in place: a squeeze page that turns clicks into subscribers, follow-up emails that run for at least a few weeks, and an offer those subscribers actually want. Without those three, they're usually a fast way to lose money.

I sell solo ads, so you'd expect me to say yes. The honest answer is "it depends", and the good news is that it depends on things you can check before you spend much.

Below is how I'd decide, the simple math to run first, and the cheapest way to get a real answer for your own offer.

When solo ads are worth it

  • You're building an email list. Solo ads send people who already read marketing emails. If your plan is to grow a list and sell to it over time, that's exactly the traffic you want.
  • Your offer fits email readers. Make money online, affiliate marketing, business opportunities, personal development, health and crypto education all do well. There's a full list in the best niches for solo ads.
  • You follow up. Most sales come from the emails people get in the days and weeks after they subscribe, not from the first page they see.
  • You want predictable costs. You know the price per click before you buy. No ad account to get banned, no bidding, no targeting to learn.
  • You track everything. With your own click tracker you can see which sellers send buyers and drop the rest.

When solo ads are not worth it

  • You sell B2B software or local services. Solo ad lists are mostly individual online buyers, not companies or people in your town.
  • You send clicks straight to an affiliate link. People who've never heard of you rarely buy on the first click. Capture the email first.
  • You have no follow-up emails yet. Set up a short sequence before you buy anything. This 7-day sequence is a good start.
  • You need profit the same day. Solo ads pay back over weeks, not hours.
  • You can't afford to lose the test money. Treat your first runs as the price of learning your numbers.

Do the math before you buy

You're really buying subscribers, not clicks. Two numbers decide whether solo ads are worth it for you:

Cost per subscriber = price per click ÷ opt-in rate

Say you pay $0.30 a click and 35% of people opt in. Each subscriber costs you about $0.86. Now the second number: how much does a subscriber earn you in their first 30 days? If you sell a $47 product and 2 in every 100 new subscribers buy it in their first month, each subscriber is worth about $0.94. That's a small profit, before any later sales.

Change one number and the answer changes. At a 15% opt-in rate the same click costs $2 per subscriber and you lose money. That's why the page you send people to matters as much as who you buy from. What makes a solo ad worth the money

Where the clicks come from

If your offer sells mainly in the US, UK, Canada and Australia, ask for the Tier-1 share in writing. Cheap clicks from countries that don't buy your offer aren't a bargain. More in Tier-1 vs Tier-2 traffic.

A seller you can check

Look for ratings from real buyers, repeat customers and a clear guarantee. Be careful with prices that look too good to be true. These red flags of a bad solo ad vendor will save you money.

Your own tracking

Never rely only on the seller's report. A free tracker shows you real clicks, opt-ins and sales per seller. Here's how to set up solo ad tracking.

A page that converts

A short, focused squeeze page with one clear promise usually beats a long one. We review funnels for free before sending traffic because a weak page wastes a good list.

Solo ads compared with other paid traffic

Facebook and Google ads can work brilliantly, but they need an ad account, careful targeting and time to learn, and make-money-online offers are often restricted. Solo ads are simpler: you buy a set number of clicks from people who read marketing emails. The trade-off is less control over who exactly sees your offer. There's a full comparison in solo ads vs Facebook ads.

How to find out for about $100

  1. Set up a squeeze page and at least 5 to 7 follow-up emails.
  2. Add your own click tracking.
  3. Buy one small run of about $100 from a seller with good ratings.
  4. Check your cost per subscriber when the run is done.
  5. Wait 30 days and add up what those subscribers earned.
  6. If they earned more than they cost, run the same seller again. If not, fix the weakest part (usually the page or the emails) before buying more.

My honest verdict

Solo ads are worth it for list builders and affiliate marketers who follow up and track their numbers. They're not worth it for local businesses, B2B sellers or anyone hoping for same-day profit. If you're not sure which side you're on, run one small, tracked test and let your own numbers decide. And if you want a second pair of eyes on your funnel first, send it over; the review is free.
