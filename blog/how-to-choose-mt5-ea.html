Score the EA out of 10. If it fails checks 1, 2, or 3, stop — that's an automatic no.

7 red flags of a scam EA

  1. "95%+ win rate" or "never loses". Impossible to sustain; usually paired with no stop loss.
  2. "No stop loss needed". Code-speak for martingale or grid recovery.
  3. Only "every-tick (lowest quality)" tester screenshots. Curve-fit results that won't repeat live.
  4. You can't backtest it. If the seller blocks the Strategy Tester, walk away.
  5. Vague strategy. "Proprietary AI algorithm" with no explanation.
  6. Pressure + fake scarcity. "Only 3 copies left" countdowns on a digital file.
  7. "Free" EA tied to one high-spread broker. You pay through the spread instead.

Green flags of a trustworthy robot

  • A real stop loss on every trade — ideally structure- or volatility-based, like the swing-structure stop used by Gold Scalpers EA.
  • Backtestable and demo-friendly — the seller wants you to test it.
  • Risk controls baked in: daily limits, drawdown guard, spread filter, sizing.
  • Single-market focus and clear, plain-English strategy.
  • A refund guarantee and ongoing updates.

5 questions to ask before you buy

  1. "Can I run this in the Strategy Tester and on demo first?" (Answer must be yes.)
  2. "What is the exact entry and exit logic?"
  3. "Where is the stop loss placed on each trade?"
  4. "Does it use martingale, grid, or averaging?" (You want a firm no.)
  5. "What's the refund policy?"

Verify the claims yourself

Never take a sales page at face value — including ours. Confirm any EA the same way we tell readers to: backtest it in MT5 on several years of data, then forward-test on demo, then go live small using the 1% risk rule. Frequently asked questions

How do I know if an MT5 EA is a scam?

Red flags: "never loses" claims, no stop loss, hidden martingale/grid, no way to backtest, and a seller who won't explain the strategy.

What's the most important thing when choosing an EA?

That you can verify it yourself — fully backtestable in MT5 and testable on demo before risking real money.

Are martingale EAs safe?

No. They look safe until one trend triggers an exponential drawdown that wipes the account.

Should an EA have a stop loss?

Yes — on every trade. "No-SL" EAs are usually hiding recovery averaging.

How much should I pay?

$100–$500 one-time for a focused single-market EA; favour one with a refund guarantee.
